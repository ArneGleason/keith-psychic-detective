# Animation working brief

These are general production suggestions, NOT verified current Kling interface instructions or model capabilities. Claude should inspect the actual account/tool options available and work within Arne's authorized credit budget. Do not invent pricing, clip limits, access or remaining credits.

## Efficient first pass

1. Inventory repository keepers and reference assets; identify missing latest pages. Read the actual images, not filenames alone.
2. Choose one short or combination; state which dialogue is original, preserved, shortened or newly proposed.
3. Make a shot list and rough timed animatic using stills and temporary speech. Judge whether the gag works before generating many variations.
4. Establish a small set of clean character keyframes without balloons, titles or comic borders. Preserve the approved design and deliberate caricature. Do not directly animate an entire six-panel page.
5. Test a close-up reaction, a two-character exchange and one physical gag. Then scale up. Log every generated clip against shot ID and source keyframe.
6. Assemble, review timing, replace only shots whose specific faults hurt comprehension. Save the working edit, audio, prompts, selected clips and rejected-test notes so another tool can continue.

## Format and performance

Suggested main-film format: landscape 16:9, with room to crop later; this is a proposal, not a pre-existing requirement. Source comics are portrait. Recompose shots rather than stretch or crop off important action. Conservative movement is welcome: eye shifts, breath, mouth motion, one hand action, slow camera push. Avoid constant floating cameras and exaggerated animation of every prop.

Keep reaction beats. A half-second pause before a character realizes something often matters more than another cut. Don't put the setup and payoff in simultaneous overlapping audio. Keep the audience able to identify who speaks without studying lip movements. For telepathy, use a consistent intimate voice treatment or audio cue; only Boggler reacts. For Major's fantasy, change lighting, music and camera confidence; the physical gesture and instruction survive the hard cut to reality. Warm ordinary room sound replaces the grand score.

Record or synthesize dialogue separately if that affords better control in the actual tools. Exact famous-actor voice imitation is not needed: character performance matters more. Provisional voice directions: Count dry and tired with occasional theatrical airs; Professor self-satisfied pedantic precision; Major warm earnest enthusiasm; Boggler tentative courteous confusion; Agatha clipped authoritative restraint; Laura sweet and carefully innocent; Rob breezy conspiratorial confidence. These are proposals, not locked casting.

## Practical shot prompt scaffold

SHOT [ID], [intended editorial duration]. Use [approved reference/keyframe] for identity and costume. [Character] at [established position] does ONE action: [specific movement]. [Other character] responds with [visible acting]. Camera [locked / modest specific movement], [framing]. Preserve [critical prop arrangement]. End on [usable held pose]. Rich tactile practical ape-mask graphic-novel look matching source; stable face, outfit, hands and lighting. No lettering, captions, new props, morphing costumes or extra characters. Sound/line, if supported in chosen workflow: [exact words].

For complex breakage, build separate before/contact/after shots. Generate the consequence rather than asking a model to solve an entire Rube Goldberg chain. Maintain the same direction of travel and insert an establishing shot. Do not let an object spontaneously appear at the moment it becomes the punchline.

## Continuity review before spending on variants

- Correct ape identity and costume? Professor has no glasses; Agatha does.
- Major stays recognizable in fantasy; ordinary outfit returns at reveal.
- Hand anatomy, feet on floor, plausible limbs and chair contact.
- Who can hear which line? No response to unheard thoughts.
- Prop screen direction, scale, window/door placement, booth seating.
- Comic text removed from animation keyframes; final captions added cleanly in edit if wanted.
- Setup visible before payoff; joke not announced by score or camera too early.
- No automatic lore expansion, new mission, or resolving ending that buries the final joke.

## Suggested deliverables from Claude

A chosen short treatment, source-page mapping, timed script/shot list, clean keyframes, short proof-of-style sequence, final edit plus versioned assets, and a small generation log with prompt/model/settings as actually used and credits if available. Ask Arne only for consequential creative or budget choices; make routine production decisions without repeatedly pausing.
