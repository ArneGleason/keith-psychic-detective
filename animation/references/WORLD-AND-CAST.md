# World and cast

## What makes it work

Four ape men with strange powers share a rundown headquarters and ordinary, undignified lives. They are not out to save the world. There is no required origin, mission, grand villain or coherent long-term purpose. The Professor is nominally the leader; that need not explain why anybody lives there. Episodes should usually make sense in any order. Small continuity rewards are welcome; exposition homework is not.

Comedy comes from vanity, misplaced confidence, petty needs, social awkwardness, and a surprising mundane use for an ability. Neighbours exploit Major's destructive touch; the Professor substitutes elaborate preparation for doing anything; Boggler treats absurd private thoughts as serious interpersonal problems; the Count's useful power creates selfish conveniences and inconveniences.

Adult, dry, sometimes rude. Swearing works best as a sharp beat. Bathroom humour can be explicit in dialogue but is stronger when the accident itself stays offscreen. Avoid generic punchline words such as 'consequences', overexplaining the gag, or repeating a point after it lands. Earnestness is important: Major is not malicious, and Laura's affection for him can be real even while she uses him.

The user especially likes elaborate heroic imagination snapping back to a mundane reality: shared dialogue and gestures make both interpretations plausible, with the reveal saved for the end. Do not label the scene a dream at the outset.

## Visual language

Rich full-colour graphic-novel/photo-comic imagery with tactile 1970s practical ape-mask character faces, shaggy hair, readable caricature expressions, detailed worn interiors and warm lighting. Maintain the appearance shown in approved comic pages. Not flat generic cartoons, not modern glossy superhero redesigns, not newspaper halftone or washed-out newsprint. The user enjoys animation precisely because stylization tolerates movement oddities better than attempted live-action realism.

Title is THE PRIMATE SUSPECTS, always complete. Comic title lettering is yellow/red on teal. Smaller episode title is separate. Do not clip letters to fit. For film, create titles in editing rather than asking video generation to redraw them.

Hands have FOUR fingers plus a thumb. Character clothing and face identities must not blend. First dialogue balloon is substantially higher than the response; this translates into unmistakable spoken turn-taking and reaction timing in animation.

## The four

### Count Napula
Brown/reddish ape, relatively short auburn hair, navy double-breasted coat and red cape/scarf. Dry, dignified, mildly theatrical, susceptible to indignity. His ornate performance voice from the insomnia episode is a special affectation, not everybody's default delivery.

When he naps, hours can pass for him and anyone nearby inside a time bubble while hardly any time passes outside. He seems always refreshed and rarely seen sleeping. He cannot simply sleep through the night to make morning arrive sooner: the external night barely advances. Someone awake inside his bubble can get hours of work done while he sleeps.

Newest approved episode adds: unusually deep sleep after an all-night caffeine mishap expands the bubble to encompass the house; its elastic boundary physically resists leaving. Inside is ordinary subjective time; outside barely progresses. Do NOT describe this as people inside being frozen, or as time travel. Do not treat rejected brainstorming about reversed time, a sleeping plumber, or automatic one-way suction as canon. The exact equations, wakeability and normal radius remain loose. Keep the effect local and comprehensible; don't invent explanations unless the gag needs one.

### Major Malfunction
Short stocky brown ape, pale steel helmet, gray double-breasted military tunic, black collar/cuffs, brass buttons, yellow cords/belt, red neck scarf, gray trousers, black boots. Earnest, eager to help, proudly credentialed by dubious authorities. His touch breaks THINGS, not merely machines. Breakage is a comic tendency, not a simulation requiring every held teacup to explode.

He daydreams himself into a dashing capable hero. Fantasy can upgrade the uniform to military space armour while retaining his recognizable face and helmet. He is the Professor's cousin. Do not rename him Major Havoc; that was a conversational slip.

### Professor Hindsight
Tall gray shaggy ape, dark muzzle, mane/beard, long ivory double-breasted coat and patterned red cravat. NO glasses, tweed jacket, or extra waist flaps: those drifted in from his mother's design and were removed. Pompous, status-conscious, gullible when flattered, wonderfully certain about useless things. Insight arrives five minutes too late. The power need not appear in every story and should not be turned into reliable foresight.

Has a Commodore 64 labelled CEREBRIAC 2001, his supposedly formidable electronic brain, really connecting him to a BBS of nerds who assume he's joking. Collects disaster souvenir plates. Spent eight years studying interviewers' habits and acquiring supplies without actually starting interviews. He isn't secretly conducting important research.

### Mind Boggler
Reddish swept shaggy hair, dark muzzle, amber eyes, burgundy coat, light blue patterned cravat. Earnest, confused by his own mental processes, susceptible to an object's appeals. Started by reading only his own mind; has heard fruit, cereal, a ceramic gnome and a goat. Broad object telepathy is available, but do not claim every object must speak or that he reads everybody's mind effortlessly.

Only he hears these thoughts. In film distinguish telepathy from audible animal noises and ordinary dialogue; other characters must not react to something they could not hear. He can whisper to a goat while waiting for something tactful to relay. Don't make him a permanently knowing wisecracker: often he is the sincere intermediary.

## Neighbours and recurring faces

- **Dr. Agatha Hindsight:** Professor's mother; eminent, uptight academic, immediately unimpressed by his bafflegab. Elderly gray ape, compact upright posture, gray bun, low fine reading glasses, brown tweed blazer with leather elbow patches, white ruffled blouse with ordinary pearl buttons, tartan skirt, sensible lace-up shoes, leather document case. ONE polished GOLD banana brooch on lapel. Not a yellow banana and no duplicate throat brooch. Her institutional visit has not needed on-page explanation. Fantasy Agatha can become a glamorous space scientist but remain recognizable.
- **Cyril / Bald Cyril:** human salvage dealer, wiry older face, sparse/unruly gray hair, green work jacket and stained brown apron. Sharp, opportunistic; turns useless inventory into Professor-bait. Shop signs and clutter matter. Early naming wandered; use Cyril for the human and Clive for the gnome.
- **Clive:** ceramic mooning garden gnome, red hat, white beard, blue coat, dropped trousers and boots. Permanently holds up the Professor's disaster-plate shelf with his hat. His thoughts are blunt and foul-mouthed; only Boggler hears them. Now sells love-life advice for five cents, Boggler translating for half the proceeds. A small taped sign on his backside reads LOVE LIFE ADVICE — 5¢. Keep ceramic material unmistakable. He is not a walking living dwarf. Prior image tools inconsistently rejected exposed ceramic anatomy; a sign is also part of the established design, not something to elaborate sexually.
- **Laura Pritchard:** adult human caricature inspired by Mary Tyler Moore as Laura Petrie, dark flipped bob, fitted blue sweater and capris. Warm, clever, apparently forgiving when Major breaks things she secretly wanted gone. Do not call her Mary.
- **Rob Pritchard:** adult human lanky Dick Van Dyke-inspired husband, cardigan, shirt/tie. Independently discovers Major's usefulness without realizing Laura does the same. Wants space for a pool table. He is Rob, not Dick. Values his chimney-sweep bobblehead with tiny brush.
- **Peg:** young adult 1950s diner waitress, mint uniform, white collar/apron/cap, waved brown hair. Savvy deadpan, knows the regulars. Can expose their mockery with a remark she could plausibly claim was innocent. Doesn't mug or announce that she caught them.
- **Unnamed hippie neighbour:** adult human, narrow long face, prominent nose, round dark glasses, messy sandy-brown hair; worn oatmeal cardigan with elbow patches, old ivory long dress with blue hem patch, Birkenstocks, goat on rope. Suspicious of unfamiliar people and 'unnatural' influences, readily forgets this when flattered. Treat that as HER hypocrisy, not a claim about a demographic. Originally queued in a bridal gown/cardigan; ordinary visits needn't use bridal costume. No canonical name yet.
- **Her goat:** tan-gray, curved horns, beard, horizontal pupils, rope collar and bell. Potty-mouthed, unimpressed. Likes her singing because it keeps foxes away.
- **Eyebrow lady:** elderly human with painted eyebrows absurdly high above her actual brow; only a background queue character so far.
- **BBS nerd:** smug but comically awkward human, big glasses, thinning fringe, small moustache, sci-fi shirt; high-tech basement with Batman/comic memorabilia. Not cool or sleek.

## Places and continuity props

Shabby headquarters with old armchairs, cluttered kitchen and worn wallpaper; fridge sign 'Days without poop fling: 1'. Professor's plate shelf and gnome, C64 'brain', impractical collections. Cyril's salvage yard next door. The Pritchards' tidy retro house. Peg's diner feels like a little 1950s world regardless of outside time period. Budget supermarket has tired linoleum and worn shelving.

Do not perpetuate temporary damage automatically: Count's terrible haircut carries from episode 4 into 5 but is not a mandatory permanent model change. Do not move seated characters between positions to keep every face unobscured; plausible occlusion is better.
