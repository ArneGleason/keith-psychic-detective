# Short-film development options — proposals, not approved scripts

## Best motion test: Special Containment (60–90 seconds)

Adapt episode 18 if the keeper is available. Clear physical business, few characters, one transformation in context, recognizably Major throughout. It proves acting, timing, masks and the fantasy/reality contrast before spending credits on an ensemble film.

Open inside a cold blue spacecraft. Major's hands press down on a trembling sample case while the scientist gives practical instructions. He treats each catch as a heroic technical challenge. Brief glimpse of something pulsing behind the window, then an appendage caught under the lid. Her instruction to tuck it in sounds terrifying; his proud commitment is absurd. He sits. Sharp sound cut: SPROING. In a warm shabby room, laundry settles around him. A bra is tangled across face and helmet. Hold long enough to see his one visible eye. Agatha's ordinary, unromantic correction ends it.

Spend time on anticipation and eyes, not repeated monster shots. Do not show the overnight case beforehand. Keep the alien suggestion cartoony, not horror gore. Use the corrected keeper's case orientation; animation can preserve a consistent physical window even if comic angles were ambiguous. This is a continuity repair in adaptation, not permission to replace the source page.

## Recommended three-minute combination: A Useful Man

Combine 09 and 14: Laura and Rob independently discover the same useful neighbour. The comedy is their parallel secrecy and Major's sincere happiness at being wanted. No mission, no villain. This uses existing relationships rather than adding cast or lore.

If the material plays better at two minutes, shorten it. Do not pad. Rob's sequence should not imply he constructed the chain: he merely knows Major nearby will suffice.

## Quieter alternative: Private Conversations (90–150 seconds)

Combine a short cereal-shop exchange with episode 16. Boggler is unfailingly respectful to voices nobody else hears; people keep misreading him. Use only ONE cereal interaction as opener, then the goat scene as main set piece. Give the goat's final silence ample room. Possible new transition: Boggler leaves the store, hears a gruff 'Finally', looks at shopping bag, then discovers it came from the goat. No origin explanation required.

This is cheaper in motion complexity: walk, stop, look, reaction, whisper. The challenge is voice ownership and deadpan timing. Maintain separate voices for Boggler and thought sources, and never make the human neighbour hear the goat's insulting clause. The final 'Don’t fucking rush me' should answer Boggler's whispered plea, not the woman's direct question.

## Later, not the first technical test: Under Pressure

Episode 19 has a strong elastic-boundary visual and a concise payoff, but needs unusually clear time geography. If adapting, show water repair outside the bubble staying motionless while interior activity continues, and establish burrito leftovers without a spoken meal recap. Leave the Count asleep. Do not add a science lecture or solve the problem after the smell joke. The episode is already crowded; extending it risks weakening it.
