# A Useful Man: retrospective, 2026-10-19

One day from the comic pages to a 2:37 animatic with placeholder voices and
eight Kling test clips. The owner's verdict closes this pass. The experiment
was worth doing; the result is not the film yet, for two reasons below.

## Owner verdicts

1. **The voice acting is terrible, and that is the main drawback of the
   approach.** The sync and picture tools work; the performance does not.
   Flat TTS placeholder lines make every scene feel dead, however good the
   mouth shapes. With good voice acting to sync to, this would work a lot
   better. No more Kling credits on dialogue shots until there are real
   performances to sync.
2. **The look drifted to photoreal.** The plates read as photographs of
   actors in ape masks on a sitcom set. The target is the look of the comic
   pages themselves: graphic illustration, realistic but painterly, a bit
   caricatured. Not a print texture, no halftone dots, not a flat cartoon.
   The cause is in our own lock block, which asks for "photographed like a
   1960s American sitcom, 35mm lens look". That is a prompt fix, not a tool
   limit.
3. Treat the whole pass as a successful experiment. Keep a lookout for tool
   improvements that would change either answer.

## What worked

- The pipeline shape: clean plate per shot, one identical lock block,
  dialogue rendered separately per line, picture and voice kept separable,
  so a redub only re-runs the dialogue shots (24 credits per Avatar line).
- Kling 4.0 Keyframes image-to-video from a plate for two-shots, physical
  gags and locked-camera reactions. Costumes, helmet and hands held.
- Kling Avatar on the ape mask: full, readable mouth shapes. Kling Lip Sync
  does not move the mask enough.
- Cost: 369 credits for 8 clips. Plates by API at about 2 minutes each.
- The test battery caught the lip-sync problem before any scale-up.

## What did not

- Placeholder TTS as performance (see verdict 1).
- Photoreal lock block (see verdict 2).
- Close-up plates kept re-adding the clown and widening to two-shots; two
  were recropped by hand.
- OpenAI credits ran out after plate P26: 10 plates never rendered, P21 and
  P23 need a clown-free regen, so 12 of 41 shots show labelled stand-ins in
  `out/animatic_v1.mp4`.

## Next pass, when it happens

**Voices first.** Options, in rough order of quality:
- Real actors: the owner and friends reading to the animatic, one line per
  file, then Avatar per line. Nothing else in the pipeline changes.
- Directed TTS with voice design and emotion control (ElevenLabs v3 class),
  one designed voice per character, lines auditioned in context.
- Performance transfer: film a person acting the line, drive the character
  from the video (Runway Act-Two class). Worth a one-shot test on the Major.

**Then the look.** Lock block v3 for `tools/plates.py`: drop "photographed",
"35mm", "real masks worn by actors"; ask for painterly graphic-novel
illustration with visible brushwork, rich colour and caricatured
proportions, "in the style of the attached page", and attach a page crop as
the first reference on every plate. Test on three plates and one Keyframes
clip to confirm motion keeps the painterly surface (Kling's Restyle tool is
the fallback if it does not).

**Finish the plates** once OpenAI credits are topped up: the 10 unrendered
plus P21/P23, then rebuild the animatic with `tools/animatic.py`.

## Tools to re-check periodically

- Talking-head and lip-sync models on non-human faces (Kling Avatar, Runway
  Act-Two, Hedra, OmniHuman-type models, Veo with native audio).
- Directed, expressive TTS and voice design; anything that takes acting
  notes per line.
- Style-locked image-to-video that keeps a painterly surface in motion.
- Video-to-video performance transfer (actor on phone camera to character).
